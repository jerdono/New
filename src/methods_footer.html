How this chart was built

No continuous observed annual record exists for most of history, so each series is an anchored reconstruction: real annual data where it exists, literature anchor points elsewhere, joined by published growth models — with the two regimes drawn differently (solid vs dashed) and uncertainty bands showing the spread of estimates and definitions. The full pipeline is src/build_dataset.py; long-form documentation with per-number citations is in docs/METHODOLOGY.md and docs/research/.

1 · Substances & materials known (cumulative)

1800–2015 follows Llanos, Leal, Luu, Jost, Stadler & Restrepo (2019, PNAS 116:12660): new compounds reported in the literature grew at a stable ~4.4%/yr from 1800, cumulating to 14,341,955 distinct compounds by 2015 (Reaxys); our curve reproduces that model exactly and extends it to 2026. Pre-1800 values are historians' order-of-magnitude anchors (Paleolithic material kits; the seven metals of antiquity; Pliny's Natural History; Lavoisier's 1789 table of 33 simple substances; ~1,000 compounds known by 1800) — wide bands, dashed line. Hollow dots show CAS Registry milestones (50M in 2009 → 100M in 2015 → 250M in 2021 → >290M in 2025), a broader "registry" definition that includes sequences, polymers and mixtures.

2 · Global material extraction (Gt/yr)

1900–2024 splices the canonical material-flow-accounting series: Krausmann et al. (2009, Ecol. Econ. 68:2696; ~7 Gt/yr in 1900 → ~59 Gt in 2005), Krausmann et al. (2018, Glob. Environ. Change 52:131; ~89 Gt in 2015) and the UNEP International Resource Panel / Schandl et al. (2024, J. Ind. Ecol.; ~105 Gt in 2024). Pre-1900 values are population × sociometabolic per-capita rates (Fischer-Kowalski & Haberl: hunter-gatherer ~0.5–1, agrarian ~3–6, industrial ~15–25 t/cap/yr), cross-checked against coal, iron and HANPP series — shown as ranges, not point estimates.

3 · Research publications per year

A piecewise-exponential model calibrated to hard counts: ~100 items/yr at the birth of the scientific journal (1665, Phil. Trans. & Journal des Sçavans); ~780,000 papers catalogued for the whole 19th century (Royal Society Catalogue of Scientific Papers); 1.35M peer-reviewed articles in 2006 (Björk et al. 2009); ~3.3M Scopus S&E articles in 2022 (NSB Indicators). Segment growth rates follow Bornmann, Haunschild & Mutz (2021, Humanit. Soc. Sci. Commun. 8:224 — overall 4.10%/yr, doubling 17.3 yr) including the documented World-War slowdowns. The band reflects the ~3× spread across database definitions of "a paper" (Scopus articles vs OpenAlex works).

4 · Global Interconnectedness Index (composite, 0–100)

A documented composite capturing how large, connected, communicative and equal the human network is — the conditions collective-brain models (Henrich; Muthukrishna & Henrich 2016) link to innovation. Geometric mean of normalized components: effective connected population, trade openness (Klasing & Milionis; PWT), urbanization (HYDE), literacy & communication reach (OWID long-run series), discounted by inequality (Milanovic extraction ratios; Bourguignon & Morrisson). Classical city-states score low on scale and cohesion; high-inequality eras are penalized explicitly. Weights, normalizations and every anchor value are documented in docs/METHODOLOGY.md — this series is an index we constructed, clearly labeled as such, not an observed quantity.

Read the fine print

Headline caveats: (i) "substance", "paper" and "material" are definition-dependent — bands show the spread; (ii) pre-1800 anchors are scholarly order-of-magnitude estimates, not censuses; (iii) annual granularity before ~1800 is honest interpolation, and is drawn dashed so it can never be mistaken for measurement; (iv) recent-year publication counts are provisional (indexing lag).
